# Review Notes — What Changed, and a Short Gap Analysis

## What this pass did

1. **Split the single 3,200-line document into 13 modules**, organized by bounded context (Sales, Customer/Membership, Catalog, Billing, Finance, plus four cross-cutting technical modules and a scope/roadmap module), each with a stated dependency list so a team can parallelize work without stepping on each other.
2. **Added a "why this module exists / why it's separate" note to the top of every module** — the original doc was strong on *rules* ("do this, not that") but light on the *reasoning* a mid-level developer needs to make good judgment calls on cases the spec didn't explicitly cover.
3. **Added fully worked, numbered examples with real rupee amounts** for the parts of this domain that are hardest to get right on the first attempt: membership freeze mechanics (Module 02), offer/plan price snapshotting (Module 03), tax and invoice-line math (Module 04), payment allocation + refund vs. credit (Module 05), and the "React → SQL" anti-pattern (Module 08). The original doc had good examples in places (e.g., §10 Offer vs Plan) — this pass extends that style everywhere it was previously just prose rules.
4. **Explained each major technical decision in plain language** — e.g., why money is stored as integers, why invoice numbering is separate from the primary key, why Commands and Queries are named differently in code, why the dashboard is built last. These were implicit in the original spec's ordering/rules but not spelled out as decisions with trade-offs.
5. **Did not change any business rule, status name, or table field** from the original — this is a reorganization and elaboration, not a redesign. Anything you already agreed with in v0.1 is preserved verbatim inside the module files.

## Gap analysis against current gym-management industry practice (Aug 2026)

A quick survey of how live gym/fitness-club software products (Zenoti, Club Automation, Gymdesk, Zen Planner, and others) describe their membership/billing modules confirms the original spec's core modeling choices are sound and current: <cite index="3-1">membership software manages the full member lifecycle — joining, recurring billing with failed-payment recovery, freezes, upgrades and plan changes, renewals, and cancellations</cite>, and <cite index="8-1">the best billing tools handle recurring charges, failed payment recovery, freeze requests, and prorate calculations, not just processing a credit card</cite>. Two points worth your explicit attention, both already partially addressed in the original doc but worth calling out clearly:

- **Freeze-aware billing is treated as a first-class concern industry-wide, not an edge case.** <cite index="3-1">A freeze that doesn't stop the billing engine correctly creates the dispute that becomes a chargeback</cite> — in your offline, no-recurring-billing context this specific chargeback risk doesn't apply, but the underlying lesson does: Module 02's `MembershipFreeze.billing_behavior` field is not optional polish, it's core to avoiding "customer says they were frozen, invoice says otherwise" disputes at the front desk.
- **Proration on upgrade/downgrade and cancellation is explicitly called out** in the original doc (§29–30) but only as "should be configurable" without a worked example. I'd treat this as the one remaining area worth a design workshop with your team before development starts — proration policy (e.g., "unused days credited at daily rate of current plan" vs. "no partial-period credit") varies more between gyms than almost anything else in this domain, and it touches Membership, Billing, and Credit (Modules 02, 04, 05) simultaneously.

Everything else in the original spec — separating Membership from Billing, snapshotting catalog prices onto invoice lines, payment allocation as its own table, refund vs. credit as distinct concepts, SQLite/Electron layering — matches how mature, current gym-software vendors structure the same problem, just without their recurring-billing/payment-gateway layer, which you've correctly scoped out for v1.

## One recommendation not in the original doc

Consider adding a **`plan_change_policy` / `proration_policy` reference table** (similar to how Lead Stage or Offer discount type is a small configurable lookup, not hard-coded) so that "how do we prorate an upgrade mid-cycle" can be a per-plan setting rather than a single global rule — this mirrors the same "make policy data, not code" pattern already used for Freeze in the original spec, and would resolve the one open configurability gap noted above. This would slot into Module 03 (Catalog) as an attribute on Membership Plan, and be read by Module 02's upgrade/downgrade logic.
